# Texture replacement trial (x2 and x4, Kokiri Forest)

Local, optional packs of enlarged textures (four variants), to judge before any full pack. Everything derived from the cartridge stays on this machine, in the shared cache folder `~/.zelda-oot-js/cache/tex/<ROM SHA-1>/` (`OOT_CACHE_DIR` moves the cache; see "Location" below), outside any worktree so that deleting a worktree loses nothing; nothing is published (the Pages build copies no pack).

**Rebuilt into the cache (2026-10-19, after the worktree copies were lost).** `dump --all`: 1,560 visits, 6,625 unique textures, 0 volatile, 303,022 graphics tasks, 101 of 101 scenes, 1,523 visits ok and 37 failed: the same numbers as the first run, in about 35 minutes (about 1.2 visits per second; each of the 4 hangs costs the 120 s timeout). 115 MiB. `upscale --variants a` (with `exclude.json`): 4,528 textures enlarged, 4,529 files with `index.json`, 66 MiB on disk (99.1 MiB as RGBA8, originals 24.8 MiB), model 393 s + 37 s; 2,097 left original (539 screen-size, 49 excluded, 1,509 seen in fewer than 3 tasks); 16 of 1,970 wrapping axes with a seam worse than plain bilinear's. Moving capture of `compare --variants a` (8 places, 0 that went from replaced back to original): 950 textures drawn, 924 with an entry, 97.3% (Hyrule Field 150 of 153, Kakariko Village 162 of 165, Market 110 of 114, Inside the Deku Tree 65 of 70, Dodongo's Cavern 132 of 136, Forest Temple 61 of 63, Zora's Domain 102 of 104, Gerudo Valley 142 of 145). The table under "Coverage" below is the earlier run.

## Location

All three commands default to `~/.zelda-oot-js/cache/tex/<ROM SHA-1>/` (`OOT_CACHE_DIR` overrides the cache folder; the SHA-1 is the one `npm run port` recorded in `dist/assets/manifest.json`, so a build of `dist/` is needed first):

- `dump/`: the dump (`<hash>.png`, `index.json`, `report.json`, `visits/`). `dump --all` saves each visit as it ends, so an interrupted run, or a later one, continues with the missing visits; `--fresh` starts over.
- `pack/<variant>/`: the packs. The dev and preview servers read this folder on every request (loopback only), so a pack built while the server runs is picked up without a restart. `OOT_TEXPACK_DIR` points the servers at another folder.
- `texpack-work/`: the model's input and output of a running `upscale`, removed when it ends.

`--out` (and `--dump`, `--pack`) still name any folder. The cache's size cap and eviction leave `tex/` alone (it is not a cache entry and is expensive to rebuild); delete `<cache>/tex/<ROM SHA-1>/` to start over. Model and output stay out of the repository (AGENTS.md).

## Hook

- `tools/tex/hash.ts`: name of a decoded texture = 64-bit hash (16 hex digits) of its tile format and texel size, its size, and its RGBA8 texels as the interpreter produced them (after the palette, which is why the palette is not hashed apart), for the texels the load commands wrote only (`GfxTextureRef.loaded` in `runtime/gfx/gfx.h`; a texel that was not written is replaced by a constant, so its stale value stays out of the key but its place stays in). Shared by the renderer and the tools.
- `tools/tex/used.ts`: which of the two textures a draw state carries its combiner reads (`texturesRead`, from the combiner selectors and the cycle type, with the second cycle's TEXEL0 and TEXEL1 swapped as `web/shader.ts` does). The interpreter decodes the texture command's tile and the next one for every state; an image the combiner never reads is not a texture of the game, so neither the renderer nor `dump` looks it up or names it.
- `web/tex-pack.ts`: fetches `/texpack/<variant>/index.json` (404 or anything not JSON: no pack) and `/texpack/<variant>/<hash>.png` on first sight of a hash. The variant is `localStorage['oot.texpack.variant']` (default `a`); `ootTexPack.pack.setVariant(name)` switches it at run time. `tools/tex/serve.ts` serves `<cache>/tex/<ROM SHA-1>/pack/` (or `OOT_TEXPACK_DIR`) from the dev and preview servers to loopback clients only, mapping only `<variant>/index.json` and `<variant>/<16 hex>.png`.
- `web/renderer.ts`: a replacement exactly 2 or 4 times the size of its texture is uploaded in its place; the tile's coordinates, origin, shift and mask periods are scaled by that factor in the uniforms, so the shader's wrap, mirror, clamp, filter mode, mip levels and anisotropy apply unchanged. Others stay original. Key **T** (or `localStorage['oot.texpack']='0'`) switches between original and pack; `globalThis.ootTexPack` is the handle the compare tool uses. With the pack off (or absent) the draw is the old path: same upload, scale 1.
- A texture whose pixels change every frame hashes differently each time and gets no entry: it stays original. `dump` reports them (see below).
- The renderer's handle `ootTexPack.record(true)` / `.recorded()` logs, per graphics task, each texture drawn as `<hash><state>` (`+` replaced, `~` in the pack but not loaded yet, `-` not in the pack), with the picture of those not in the pack; `compare` uses it for the moving capture.

## Tools (`npm run tex -- <command>`)

- `dump`: plays the scripted new game headless to Link's house under control (frame 19,000), loads Kokiri Forest (entrance 238, 3,000 video frames with A taps for the cutscene, then a stick walk), then the six interior entrances the forest's exit list names (626, 193, 201, 156, 1075, 1079; `docs/re/display-lists.md` section 9), 700 frames each. Writes every unique texture the combiner read to `out/texdump/<hash>.png`, `index.json` (size, tile format, wrap/mirror/clamp per axis, alpha kind, frames seen, scenes, texels not loaded, number of distinct pictures the key stands for) and `report.json`. Churn rule: a group (size and tile format) is churning when it is drawn in at least 20 graphics tasks and has new pixels in at least half of them; its textures seen in at most 5 tasks are marked volatile and skipped.
- `dump --all [--frames 600] [--entrances 238,626] [--fresh] [--hang 120]`: the whole game (`tools/tex/dump-all.ts`). For the first entry of each scene and spawn pair of the entrance table, as child and as adult, by day and by night, it forces the entrance (as the scene sweep does), taps A for 150 video frames (cutscene, loading, actors, animated textures), then holds the stick through eight headings of 50 frames (a circle of walking: the camera trails Link, so it turns with him; it is not a direct camera turn, and Link may leave through an exit, in which case the textures count for the scene the game is in at that task). A fresh game replays the scripted new game after every visit that fails. Each visit is saved to `out/texdump/visits/<scene>-<spawn>-<age>-<day|night>.json` (records and group counts) when it ends and its pictures go to `out/texdump/<hash>.png` first, so an interrupted run (Ctrl+C finishes the visit in progress) continues with the missing visits; `--fresh` forgets them. The run is a supervisor and a worker process: a worker that prints nothing for `--hang` seconds (a guest loop that never yields cannot be interrupted from inside) is killed, the visit it was in is saved as `hang`, and a new worker continues. `index.json` merges all visits: per texture the frames (summed), scenes, ages and times it was seen in, the widest edge treatment and the union of reach; `visits` lists every visit with its status. The churn rule runs over all visits together (group counts added; a texture already seen in an earlier visit is not new pixels), so a texture seen in at most 5 tasks in all, in a churning group, is volatile. `report.json` has the counts (textures, volatile, scenes visited and missing, visits ok and failed) and each failed visit with its status and reason (`trap`, `halted`, `left-play-state`, `not-reached`: the scene at video frame 60 is not the one the table names); a failed visit keeps the textures it drew.
- **Age.** The flag at `0x801183D4` alone does not make an adult run: the overlay of file 35 copies the byte at play context `+0x11DE8` into it when the state ends (`lbu 7656` and `sw` at `0x808496DC`-`0x808496E8`), which undoes the write. Observed: entry 238 with only the flag written loads entry 236 (scene 84); with the flag and the byte at 0, the variant word reads 2 by day and 3 by night and the scene is 85. `dump --all` writes both. The scene sweep (`tools/sweep`) writes only the flag, so its adult runs are child runs of the entry two lower.
- `upscale`: enlarges every non-volatile texture of at most 128 px a side seen in at least 3 tasks into four packs, `<cache>/tex/<ROM SHA-1>/pack/<variant>/`: **a** x2, 50% model + 50% bilinear; **b** x2, model only; **c** x4, model only; **d** x4, 75% model + 25% bilinear. Per texture: padding of 16 texels (wrap, mirror or clamp per axis, from the tiles that sampled it); colour under fully transparent texels bled from visible neighbours; the model enlarges the opaque colour picture; the result is cropped back and mixed with plain bilinear by the variant's share. **Alpha never goes through the model**: it is the padded alpha enlarged by plain bicubic (Catmull-Rom) filtering and left as a gradient, so the game's alpha compare (and the filter at the edge) meets the same kind of ramp as with the original and the silhouette keeps its place (an earlier version cut it at 128, which shrank cut-outs against the original's 32/255 test). Transparent texels then take the colour of their nearest visible neighbour (no halo). Gray textures stay gray. Each wrapping axis is checked against the plain bilinear enlargement of the same texture (seam ratio). `index.json` of a pack records model, file hashes, settings, texture memory (RGBA8 bytes, 4/3 of that with mips) and build time.
- `compare`: headless Chrome, game in Kokiri Forest from save state 1 (a copy of the saves folder), six places by held arrow keys (place `kokiri`; the other places warp from the state to an entrance, see "Full pack" below); at each the frame is frozen and drawn by the page's renderer with the original textures, with each variant, and with the originals again (to check it returns to the same picture). Writes `out/tex-compare/view-N-*-<original|a|b|c|d>.png`, a `bush` (views 3, 4, 6) and a `wall` (views 1, 2, 4) close-up (1:1, 480x360, centred on the on-screen area drawn with the plants' leaf texture and with the cliff face textures; 3D draws only, so the HUD's minimap is not picked) per variant, and `index.html`: a swipe slider per picture, two selectors (left and right) that pick the original or any variant, and a table of memory and times.


## Full dump (`dump --all`, 600 frames per visit)

The run on this machine (about 50 min of CPU, one process; the 6 visits that hang cost 120 s each) covers 101 scenes in 390 scene and spawn pairs, 1,560 visits (the 1,556 entries hold 37 that name no scene: 20-39, 71-74, 118-121, 148-151, 182, 1312-1315):

- **6,625 unique textures** (`<cache>/tex/<ROM SHA-1>/dump/*.png`, 115 MiB with the visit files), **0 volatile**: no group reached the churn rule over 303,022 graphics tasks, so every texture is kept for upscaling.
- **Scenes visited: 101 of 101** with at least one visit that reached its scene; 1,523 of 1,560 visits ok.
- **Failed visits (37, each keeps the textures it drew):**
  - `hang` (the worker is killed after 120 s without output): scene 7 spawn 2 and scene 92 spawn 8 (the first as child and adult, day and night; the second as child only). They add no textures; other spawns of both scenes are ok.
  - `trap`: scene 6 spawn 4, all four variants, `PI access to unmapped cartridge address 0x0096e03c`; scene 93 spawn 18 as child, day and night, `thread 0xffed0000 is runnable but was never created`.
  - `not-reached` (27): the scene at video frame 60 is another one than the table names (a cutscene or an entrance chain that moves on, mostly to scene 81 or 85); the textures count for the scene that was drawn. `report.json` lists each.

## Findings

- Texture memory (443 textures, RGBA8; 2.4 MiB as originals): a and b 9.4 MiB (4x), c and d 37.7 MiB (16x); with mip chains 12.6 and 50.3 MiB. Build time on this machine: x2 model 38 s and x4 model 37 s for the 443 textures (CPU, numpy), plus 3 s (x2) or 10 s (x4) of padding, blending, alpha, checks and PNG writing per variant.
- **Why textures had dozens of names.** The earlier hook hashed every image the interpreter decoded for a state: the texture command's tile and the next one. A draw that reads one texture still carries the decode of the other tile, whose descriptor was set for an earlier draw and whose texture memory has moved on. Example (frame 2 after loading state 1, task at `0x8018C998`): tile 1 is a 32x32 RGBA16 tile at TMEM word 256 (`F5101100 01094250`, loaded from `0x2570A8` by `F3000000 073FF100`), the draws that follow read only tile 0, and a `LOADTLUT` at tile 7, word 256 (`F5000100 07000000`, `F0000000 073FC000`: 256 entries = 512 bytes) writes over the first 8 rows of tile 1's region. Decoded, those rows are palette bytes shown as noise (checked: they differ from RAM at `0x2570A8` in rows 0 to 7 only, RAM there stays unchanged over 120 frames). The 17 families of the earlier dump (32x32 textures whose upper rows were noise and whose lower half was the real picture) are consistent with this and are gone from the new dump; gating the lookup on what the combiner reads removes these images: 617 unique decodes in the dump become 452, none has more than one picture, and in the moving capture the textures without an entry fell from 33 to 4.
- **Loaded texels.** The interpreter also reports, per image, which texels the loads wrote (`runtime/gfx/dl.c`, "Loaded texels"): each TMEM byte remembers the load command that wrote it, a tile's window is the loads between its last two SetTile commands that followed a load, a palette load does not count as a texel load, and the key leaves out texels outside the window. In the Kokiri walk and the six interiors no read texture has an unloaded texel (`unloaded` is 0 in all 452 records), so this part of the key does not change any name here; it is there for tiles taller than their block (unit test: `tools/gfx/dl.test.ts`) and costs nothing when everything was loaded. The key replaced the lower-half fallback (`hashLowerHalf`, `families.ts`, the `aliases` of the pack index), which is gone.
- **Moving capture** (`npm run tex -- compare`: save state 1, the same walk of four arrow-key legs of 4 s, played once to request the replacements and once recorded; variant a; 318 graphics tasks): 108 distinct textures drawn, 104 with an entry in the pack, **0 that went from replaced back to original**, 4 without an entry. Before (the previous tree and dump, run on this machine, same walk): 232 textures seen, 39 without an entry with the lower-half keys off and 33 with them on, 36 and 32 of them not in the dump. The four left are not in the dump, because the dump plays a new game in Link's house and the capture a save with a different HUD and inventory; none is in the dump and missing from the pack (`out/tex-compare/moving.json` lists them with reasons):
  - an 8x16 I8 (the "6" of the HUD counter), drawn every task;
  - a 48x16 IA4 (the "Attaque" button label, localised text), drawn in 289 tasks;
  - a 32x64 I8 and a 32x64 IA16 (item pictures of the HUD/pause scene), 19 tasks each.
  Replaced at the first draw after the request arrives (~a few tasks): `~` entries, which are not counted as flicker.
- Pictures of the pack are still those of the first member seen; with one picture per key that is the only one.
- Seams: variant a has none worse than plain bilinear; b 1, c 5, d 5 of 87 wrapping axes (the model's own sharpening at the seam, not padding).

## Model and settings used

Third-party model, run locally, weights not committed: `realesr-animevideov3-x2` from the official release `xinntao/Real-ESRGAN` v0.2.5.0, asset `realesrgan-ncnn-vulkan-20220424-windows.zip` (zip SHA-256 `abc02804e17982a3be33675e4d471e91ea374e65b70167abc09e31acb412802d`), extracted into `~/.zelda-oot-js/tools/realesrgan-ncnn-20220424/`. The x2 and x4 model files are read by `tools/tex/model_run.py` (numpy; Python 3.12 venv at `~/.zelda-oot-js/tools/venv` with `numpy` and `pillow`). Why not the release's own executable: its Vulkan build lost the device on the RTX 4060 Ti (`vkWaitForFences failed -4`), and the `ncnn` Python package returned different, partly garbage output on every run of this model; the numpy reader matches the clean ncnn output (mean difference 0.06/255) and is deterministic. This model is not a general-purpose one: it is a compact network for cartoon video; the release's x2 variant is its x4 network followed by a bicubic halving (end of its param file). At full strength (variant b) it draws ink-like outlines on bark and eyes; variant a mixes it 50/50 with bilinear.

## Full pack, variant a (`upscale --variants a` over the full dump)

`npm run tex -- upscale --variants a` builds only variant a and leaves the other packs of the pack folder as they are (before, every run emptied it). `tools/tex/exclude.json` (hash to reason) lists textures that stay original; the upscaler honours it and `compare` reports them as "excluded".

- **Counts** (6,625 textures in the dump): **4,528 in the pack**; 2,097 left original: 1,509 seen in fewer than 3 graphics tasks, 539 larger than 128 px on a side (tall strips included), 49 excluded (below).
- **Size:** 99.1 MiB RGBA8 for the 4,528 textures (their originals: 24.8 MiB, so 4x), 132 MiB with mip chains; the PNG files take 66 MB on disk. Seams: 16 of 1,970 wrapping axes are worse than plain bilinear's (the forest trial had none).
- **Build time** on this machine (CPU, numpy, one process): model 564 s + 46 s of padding, blending, alpha, checks and PNG writing = 610 s (a first run under load: 859 s + 99 s).
- **Animated textures.** The dump's churn rule marks none of the 6,625 volatile, which does not mean none animate:
  - Scene 26 ("Off Inside Ganon's Castle (5)") draws **1,421 different 64x64 I8 pictures** (1,449 textures seen in at most 2 tasks, all in that scene): a picture generated every frame. The churn rule misses it because its group (64x64 I8) is shared with the static textures of other scenes, so fewer than half of the group's tasks carry new pixels. They stay original only through the "fewer than 3 tasks" rule.
  - Animation by swapped pictures (the smoke and dust puffs of the Forest Temple, 32x32 and 32x24 I8 sequences) gives each frame its own hash; each frame is in the pack as its own enlarged picture, so nothing is frozen. In the moving captures **no texture went from replaced back to original** (0 in all 8 places); draws of frames whose replacement had not arrived yet (`~`, 0 to 643 draws per place) are drawn original until it does.
  - Animation by scrolling (water, lava) moves tile offsets, not texels: the key does not change and the enlarged picture scrolls like the original.
  - The one real risk is a key that stands for several pictures: the dump records 10 such textures (`pictures` above 1: 2 to 9 pictures each, 5 of them seen in over 1,000 tasks). A pack entry would hold the first picture only, so they are in `exclude.json` and stay original.
- **Spot-check** (`npm run tex -- compare --variants a`: headless Chrome, save state 1, then a warp to the entrance): Hyrule Field 381, Kakariko Village 219, Market 177, Inside the Deku Tree 0, Dodongo's Cavern 4, Forest Temple 361 (the adult dungeon, entered with the save's child Link), Zora's Domain 264, Gerudo Valley 279; two views each, `arrival` and `ahead` (2.5 s of the up arrow). Writes `out/tex-compare/index.html` (swipe slider, original or a, and the coverage table), `moving-<place>.json` and `sheet-<place>-N.png` (each texture a place drew, once: original left with hard pixels, replacement right, hash under it, red frame: no pack entry).
  - In every place the pack replaces textures: 1.5% to 15% of the pixels differ by more than 8/255 (the Market arrival most).
  - **Looks worse: noise-like gray detail textures.** I and I8 pictures that are mostly grain (rock and cliff detail layers; hashes 053dfeb0d179f397, 9c1b1eb2a1b9e656, 7c215be9abc94b8d, dba565f27652b75f, fae2b26f0f9d866b): the model reads the grain as structure and draws ink-like worm outlines (seen zoomed 3x against the original; in the Dodongo's Cavern cliff the blotches show in the whole picture). Rule used to list them: format I (4 or 8 bit), shorter side at least 32 texels, mean absolute difference of neighbouring texels at least 24/255, and the same at two texels apart at most 1.35 times that (grain keeps its contrast at every distance; edges and carvings lose it). It selects 41 textures; the reliefs and soft blobs that fall outside it were looked at (a sun relief tile, a hieroglyph strip, a spider web, smoke puffs) and the pack is as good as or better than the original there. All 41 are in `exclude.json` with their reason.
  - **Not fixed, to look at:** the final colour bleed (`bleedColour`) also gives I and I8 texels of intensity 0 (alpha 0) the colour of their nearest brighter neighbour, so a black mask region turns grey or white in the colour channels while alpha stays 0 (a grey halo around an I8 strip, `ea957d782cc77b92`; the blob masks are white all over in the colour channels). A combiner that reads the colour of such a texture and does not blend by its alpha would show white where the original shows black. No picture of the spot-check showed it; not tested beyond them.
  - **Round trip:** in 4 of the 16 views of the first full run (Hyrule Field ahead, Dodongo's Cavern ahead, Forest Temple arrival, Gerudo Valley ahead) the original textures drawn again after the pack gave another picture than the first original draw; not investigated (the six forest views of the trial all matched).
  - The Market warp once left the page drawing nothing (0 graphics tasks); `compare` loads a place a second time when that happens. `web/tex-pack.ts` counted `pending` as requests minus arrivals, which went negative after `setVariant` while fetches were in flight and never reached 0; it counts requests without an arrival now.
